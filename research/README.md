# StayJustIn Research Library

This folder is the evidence side of StayJustIn.

The goal is simple: the article is not the center. The research is.

Every agenda packet, budget PDF, staff report, contract, map, email, photo, meeting minute, or video note should be processed into reusable intelligence before it becomes public content.

## Operation Backlog Workflow

Use this pipeline for every document:

1. Original document
2. Executive summary
3. Key facts
4. Money
5. Decisions
6. Timeline
7. Resident impact
8. Questions still open
9. Follow-up opportunities
10. Issue tags
11. Confidence notes
12. Reusable content ideas

## Confidence Labels

Use these labels on facts, claims, and questions.

| Label | Meaning | Use When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 🟢 Confirmed | Directly supported by an official document, public record, meeting video, or verified source. | The fact can be published with confidence. |
| 🟡 Needs Follow-Up | Mentioned or implied, but incomplete, unclear, or missing context. | The fact needs a question, email, records request, or meeting follow-up. |
| 🔴 Unverified | A resident claim, rumor, unofficial statement, or unresolved allegation. | Do not publish as fact. Track it until confirmed or disproven. |

## Five Reusable Outputs

Every processed document should create more than one article idea. Aim to produce:

1. Research summary
2. Facts for an issue hub
3. Article ideas
4. Facebook or Nextdoor post angle
5. Substack topic or follow-up thread

## Folder Strategy

Research is organized by issue, not just by meeting date. This makes it easier to connect documents over time.

Current issue folders:

- `budget/`
- `street-sweeping/`
- `del-norte/`

- `parks/`
- `public-safety/`
- `animal-services/`
- `council-agendas/`
- `contracts/`
- `ceqa-documents/`
- `emails/`

A document can belong to more than one issue. When that happens, keep the original source in the most logical folder and cross-reference it in the other related folder notes.

## Naming Convention

Use plain names that sort well:

```txt
YYYY-MM-DD-topic-source-type.md
```

Examples:

```txt
2026-05-19-special-events-budget-staff-report.md
2026-04-07-street-sweeping-council-action.md
2026-05-05-city-yard-brandywine-ena.md
```

## Research Rule

Do not rush a document straight into an article.

Process it first. Tag it. Extract the money. Capture the promises. Write down what is still unanswered. Then decide whether it deserves an article, hub update, social post, Substack, or simply stays as supporting evidence.
